\begin{abstract}
Decoding open-vocabulary natural language from non-invasive electroencephalography (EEG) is a pivotal frontier for assistive brain-computer interfaces (BCIs). However, recent critiques and empirical audits have exposed three fundamental vulnerabilities in prevailing EEG-to-text architectures: (1) reliance on teacher-forcing evaluation, wherein autoregressive language models memorize training corpora rather than conditioning on brain signals; (2) implicit \textit{signal-length leakage}, whereby variable-length sequence padding reveals target sentence lengths even under pure Gaussian noise; and (3) severe cross-subject degradation caused by individual skull-impedance shifts and flat sensor representations. To overcome these challenges, we propose \textbf{RUSH-EEG2Text}, a two-stage connectome-guided discrete brain-to-LLM decoding framework evaluated across both standard cross-subject reading and strict double zero-shot (unseen subject and sentence) regimes on compact unified-memory AI hardware. First, we map 105 high-density scalp electrodes via their 3D stereotactic coordinates into 12 anatomical cortical regions of interest (ROIs), extracting multi-stage cortical reading dynamics, 48-pair hemispheric lateralization, and 500-Hz cross-spectral functional connectomes comprising Weighted Phase Lag Index (wPLI), Imaginary Coherence (iCoh), and Log-Euclidean Symmetric Positive Definite (SPD) tangent vectors. Second, we eliminate length leakage via a continuous $[0,1]$ progress Fourier positional encoding coupled with a fixed-query Perceiver resampler and a zero-initialized Late Connectome Bottleneck Fusion adapter. Third, we introduce an un-inflated coarse-to-fine Exponential Moving Average Residual Vector Quantizer (EMA-RVQ) trained jointly under multi-positive cross-subject InfoNCE and brain-to-brain neural invariance constraints, before bridging quantized neural prefixes to sequence-to-sequence (\texttt{BART-large}) or causal (\texttt{Qwen2.5-7B}) decoders with scheduled target-word dropout. On strict unseen-subject 400-way retrieval (\texttt{task1-SR}), Stage~1 elevates Mean Reciprocal Rank (MRR) from $6.40\%$ to $\mathbf{22.22\%}$ ($\text{R@10} = \mathbf{44.30\%}$, median rank $= \mathbf{15.0}$) while suppressing pure Gaussian noise to true random chance ($1.42\%$ MRR, $+\mathbf{20.80\%}$ sanity gap); diagnostic controls confirm that real EEG outperforms gaze-only features by $+9.67\%$ MRR ($+22.54\%$ R@10) and EEG-shuffled controls by $+3.06\%$ MRR ($+9.33\%$ R@10). Scaling across all four reading tasks of ZuCo 1.0 and ZuCo 2.0 (18,276 trials, 30 subjects, 1,313-sentence candidate bank) achieves consistent generalization across all four held-out test subjects ($\text{R@10} = \mathbf{56.79 \pm 2.44\%}$, $\text{MRR} = \mathbf{23.09 \pm 1.64\%}$, median rank $= \mathbf{8.50 \pm 0.58}$ vs.\ $0.82\%$ noise MRR). Under strict teacher-forcing-free decoding across 2,717 unseen-subject trials, Stage~2 achieves $\mathbf{19.60\%}$ BLEU-1, $\mathbf{9.89\%}$ BLEU-4 ($11.1\times$ higher than prior cross-subject state of the art), $\mathbf{19.58\%}$ ROUGE-1 F1, and $\mathbf{90.85\%}$ WER, outperforming pure Gaussian noise by $+\mathbf{10.83\%}$ $\Delta\text{BLEU-1}$ and $+\mathbf{9.46\%}$ $\Delta\text{BLEU-4}$ ($23.0\times$ over noise). When controlled on the identical \texttt{BART-large} (406M) backbone and single-task \texttt{task1-SR}, RUSH-EEG2Text achieves $\mathbf{16.38\%}$ BLEU-1 and $\mathbf{0.97\%}$ BLEU-4, outperforming prior BART-based baselines under non-teacher-forced generation.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\IEEEPARstart{R}{econstructing} open-vocabulary natural language directly from non-invasive brain recordings such as electroencephalography (EEG) and magnetoencephalography (MEG) promises a paradigm shift in assistive communication for individuals with severe neuromotor impairments, locked-in syndrome, or post-stroke aphasia~\cite{wang2022open, defossez2023decoding, wang2026genai_bci}. Compared with conventional discriminative brain-computer interface (BCI) paradigms---such as motor imagery, P300 spellers, and steady-state visually evoked potentials (SSVEPs), which restrict users to low-bitrate closed command sets---generative language BCIs aim to translate continuous spatiotemporal cortical dynamics into unconstrained semantic text~\cite{wang2024cetmae, li2025brainecho}.

Driven by pretrained sequence-to-sequence and causal large language models (LLMs), pioneer frameworks such as EEG-to-Text~\cite{wang2022open}, DeWave~\cite{duan2023dewave}, C-SCL~\cite{feng2023cscl}, and CET-MAE~\cite{wang2024cetmae} established word- and sentence-level decoding pipelines on naturalistic reading corpora~\cite{hollenstein2018zuco, hollenstein2020zuco2}. Despite encouraging benchmark numbers under teacher forcing, recent diagnostic investigations---notably Jo \textit{et al.}~\cite{jo2024are} and Li \textit{et al.} (\textit{BrainECHO})~\cite{li2025brainecho}---have uncovered three critical methodological bottlenecks that impede clinically trustworthy EEG-to-LLM translation:

\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Teacher-Forcing Over-Dominance and Pure-Noise Memorization:} Most BART-based decoders condition each token prediction on ground-truth preceding tokens during evaluation. Under strict teacher-forcing-free (\textit{TF-free}) free-running generation, performance collapses dramatically, and several unconstrained LLM adapters produce nearly identical BLEU scores whether fed real human EEG or pure Gaussian noise $\mathcal{N}(0, \mathbf{I})$~\cite{jo2024are, li2025brainecho}.
    \item \textbf{Implicit Signal-Length and Fixation-Count Leakage:} Even when teacher forcing is removed, padding variable-length word-fixation sequences (or variable-duration audio/EEG segments) with standard integer positional indices ($0, 1, \dots, L_{\text{seq}}-1$) implicitly reveals the exact number of fixated words to the decoder~\cite{li2025brainecho}. Our empirical audit demonstrates that under integer positional encoding, a pure Gaussian noise input still achieves $3.2\times$ random-chance retrieval accuracy solely by exploiting sequence-mask length correlations.
    \item \textbf{Cross-Subject Covariate Shift and Flat Sensor Representations:} Prior text-evoked EEG encoders flatten 105 scalp channels across 8 frequency bands into an unstructured $840$-dimensional vector extracted from a single eye-fixation window (\textit{Gaze Duration}, GD) in left-to-right lexical order~\cite{wang2022open, wang2024cetmae}. Such flat representations discard 3D cortical geometry, ignore chronological eye-regression scanpaths, conflate initial visual recognition (\textit{First Fixation Duration}, FFD) with syntactic reanalysis (\textit{Total Reading Time}, TRT), and remain highly vulnerable to inter-subject skull-conductivity and $1/f$ spectral-slope variations~\cite{song2024eegmacs}.
\end{enumerate}

While \textit{BrainECHO}~\cite{li2025brainecho} recently demonstrated that discrete vector quantization (VQ) suppresses session noise in aurally evoked brain-to-spectrogram translation via Whisper, text-evoked and inner-speech reading paradigms lack external acoustic spectrograms to serve as intermediate reconstruction targets. Moreover, vanilla Euclidean VQ codebooks suffer from severe \textit{codebook collapse} (perplexity $< 2.5$) or residual norm explosion when applied directly to high-dimensional cross-subject EEG embeddings.

To bridge these gaps, we propose \textbf{RUSH-EEG2Text}, a length-invariant, connectome-guided discrete brain-to-LLM decoding framework. Rather than treating scalp EEG as flat channel vectors, RUSH-EEG2Text integrates anatomical 3D cortical parcellation, multi-stage oculomotor-aligned neural dynamics, phase-lagged functional connectivity, and Riemannian manifold geometry into a hierarchical discrete neural tokenizer aligned with a modern 7B causal LLM. Our principal contributions are fourfold:

\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Multi-Stage 3D Cortical ROI and Riemannian-Phase Connectome Representation:} Utilizing stereotactic 3D electrode coordinates, we parcellate 105 scalp channels into 12 canonical language and association ROIs with artifact-resilient channel masking and unsupervised per-subject robust calibration. We jointly extract four-stage reading windows ($\text{FFD}, \text{GD}, \text{TRT}, \text{TRT}-\text{FFD}$), 48-pair homologous hemispheric asymmetries, chronological gaze scanpaths, pupil/oculomotor cognitive-load indices, and 500-Hz cross-spectral functional networks combining Weighted Phase Lag Index (wPLI)~\cite{vinck2011wpli}, Imaginary Coherence (iCoh)~\cite{nolte2004icoh}, and Log-Euclidean SPD covariance tangent vectors~\cite{arsigny2006logeuclidean, barachant2013riemannian}.
    \item \textbf{Zero-Leakage Scanpath Encoding and Late Connectome Bottleneck Fusion:} We replace integer positional indices with continuous $[0,1]$ scanpath-progress Fourier embeddings paired with a fixed-query Perceiver resampler, mathematically eliminating fixation-count length leakage. Furthermore, we identify and resolve \textit{early edge overfitting} via a zero-initialized \textit{Late Connectome Bottleneck Fusion} adapter that enriches resampled node queries with global connectome tokens without corrupting token-level spatial representations.
    \item \textbf{Un-Inflated Coarse-to-Fine EMA-RVQ with Multi-Positive Cross-Subject Alignment:} We design an L2-sphere-initialized Euclidean Exponential Moving Average Residual Vector Quantizer (EMA-RVQ) with dead-code revival and quantizer dropout, achieving high active codebook diversity (perplexity $> 84$) and negligible quantization loss ($\Delta\text{MRR} < 1.7\%$). Stage~1 is trained with cognitive-load-weighted multi-positive InfoNCE and a cross-subject brain-to-brain invariance loss that eliminates false-negative collisions when multiple subjects read identical sentences.
    \item \textbf{Rigorous Unseen-Subject \& Pure-Noise Sanity Verification:} Across 14 systematic Stage~1 architectural ablations (\texttt{task1-SR}) and full-scale 4-task training across 18,276 trials (30 unique subjects across ZuCo 1.0 and ZuCo 2.0) on a single 128-GB unified-memory NVIDIA DGX Spark workstation, RUSH-EEG2Text achieves $\mathbf{56.09\%}$ R@10 and median rank $\mathbf{9.0}$ out of 1,313 unique sentences across 4 held-out test subjects ($\text{MRR}=\mathbf{23.14\%}$ vs.\ $0.77\%$ under Gaussian noise), and establishes a new cross-subject teacher-forcing-free generation state of the art ($\mathbf{19.60\%}$ BLEU-1, $\mathbf{9.89\%}$ BLEU-4, $+\mathbf{10.83\%}$ $\Delta\text{BLEU-1}$ and $+\mathbf{9.46\%}$ $\Delta\text{BLEU-4}$ over pure noise).
\end{itemize}

\section{Related Work}

\subsection{Non-Invasive Brain-to-Text Decoding with Pretrained LLMs}
Early non-invasive neural speech and text decoding studies focused on closed-vocabulary classification of syllables, words, or retrieval over short 3-second segments using contrastive wav2vec~2.0 alignment~\cite{defossez2023decoding}. Wang and Ji~\cite{wang2022open} pioneered open-vocabulary EEG-to-text generation on the ZuCo reading benchmark by projecting word-level 8-band Hilbert envelope features into a pretrained BART encoder-decoder. Subsequent works improved representation alignment: DeWave~\cite{duan2023dewave} introduced discrete codex encoding to reduce reliance on eye-fixation markers; C-SCL~\cite{feng2023cscl} employed curriculum semantic-aware contrastive learning to mitigate subject shifts; Amrani \textit{et al.}~\cite{amrani2024deep} enhanced deep subject-independent encoders; CET-MAE~\cite{wang2024cetmae} combined 75\% masked EEG-text autoencoding with multi-stream contrastive alignment, concatenating holistic sentence-level EEG tokens to compensate for skipped word fixations; and LURS~\cite{park2026lurs} introduced latent-level uncertainty refinement to stabilize cross-subject rollout.

However, Jo \textit{et al.}~\cite{jo2024are} demonstrated that teacher-forced evaluation masks catastrophic failure during free-running inference and that models lacking strict input-grounded regularization hallucinate fluent text from random noise. While \textit{BrainECHO}~\cite{li2025brainecho} addressed teacher-forcing dependence for audio-evoked EEG/MEG by reconstructing quantized Mel spectrograms for Whisper, its reliance on ground-truth acoustic targets prevents direct application to silent natural reading or inner-speech BCIs, and its authors explicitly noted residual sentence-length padding correlations as an open limitation. RUSH-EEG2Text directly resolves both open problems without requiring audio stimuli.

\subsection{Cortical Functional Connectivity and Riemannian Geometry in BCIs}
Raw scalp potentials are heavily distorted by volume conduction through cerebrospinal fluid and individual skull thickness, causing dramatic covariate shifts across subjects and recording centers~\cite{song2024eegmacs}. Phase-lagged spectral estimators---specifically Imaginary Coherence~\cite{nolte2004icoh} and Weighted Phase Lag Index (wPLI)~\cite{vinck2011wpli}---isolate non-zero-lag inter-regional communication by discarding instantaneous ($\Delta\phi = 0$) volume-conducted artifacts. Complementarily, Symmetric Positive Definite (SPD) spatial covariance matrices projected onto the Riemannian Log-Euclidean tangent space~\cite{arsigny2006logeuclidean, barachant2013riemannian} capture affine-invariant coupled signal strength across brain networks and have recently proven highly effective for cross-center brain disorder diagnosis in EEG-MACS~\cite{song2024eegmacs}. To the best of our knowledge, RUSH-EEG2Text is the first framework to unify 3D ROI multi-stage oculomotor power, phase-lagged spectral connectivity, and Riemannian SPD tangent geometry for open-vocabulary EEG-to-LLM decoding.

\section{Methodology}

\subsection{Problem Formulation}
Let $\mathcal{D} = \{(\mathcal{E}_i, \mathcal{O}_i, W_i, s_i, \tau_i)\}_{i=1}^{N}$ denote a multi-subject, multi-task corpus of $N$ sentence-reading trials recorded from subjects $s_i \in \mathcal{S}$ across reading paradigms $\tau_i \in \{\text{SR}, \text{NR}, \text{TSR}\}$. Each trial comprises: (i) high-density 105-channel scalp EEG features across $L_i$ fixated words plus continuous 500-Hz sentence waveforms $\mathbf{X}_{\text{raw}} \in \mathbb{R}^{105 \times T_i}$; (ii) synchronized eye-tracking metadata $\mathcal{O}_i$ (chronological fixation positions, fixation counts, pupil dilation, saccade kinematics, and word omission rates); and (iii) ground-truth open-vocabulary word tokens $W_i = (w_{i,1}, \dots, w_{i,M_i})$. Under a strict \textit{subject-independent} split ($\mathcal{S}_{\text{train}} \cap \mathcal{S}_{\text{val}} = \emptyset$ and $\mathcal{S}_{\text{train}} \cap \mathcal{S}_{\text{test}} = \emptyset$) and \textit{teacher-forcing-free} inference, our goal is to learn a discrete neural tokenizer $\mathcal{Q}_\theta$ and causal LLM decoder $\mathcal{G}_\phi$ that generate $\hat{W}_i = \mathcal{G}_\phi(\mathcal{Q}_\theta(\mathcal{E}_i, \mathcal{O}_i, \tau_i))$ without access to any ground-truth text tokens or target sequence lengths at test time.

\subsection{Anatomical 12-ROI Parcellation and Tri-Modal Neuro-Connectome Extraction}

\subsubsection{Artifact Masking and Unsupervised Per-Subject Calibration}
In high-density EGI HydroCel recordings, transient electrode pop or local muscle artifacts corrupt individual channels within a sentence, marked as \texttt{NaN} in band-power summaries. Rather than discarding entire trials or corrupting spatial averages with zero-imputation, we construct a binary channel validity mask $\mathbf{m}_{\text{ch}} \in \{0,1\}^{105}$ per sentence. Prior to regional aggregation, we perform a strictly unsupervised per-subject robust calibration across all unlabeled trials of subject $s$:
\begin{equation}
    \tilde{x}_{c,b}^{(\omega)} = \operatorname{clip}\!\left(\frac{x_{c,b}^{(\omega)} - \operatorname{med}_{s}(x_{c,b}^{(\omega)})}{\operatorname{IQR}_{s}(x_{c,b}^{(\omega)}) / 1.349}, -6.0, 6.0\right),
\end{equation}
for channel $c \in \{1,\dots,105\}$, frequency band $b \in \{\theta_1, \theta_2, \alpha_1, \alpha_2, \beta_1, \beta_2, \gamma_1, \gamma_2\}$, and reading stage $\omega \in \{\text{FFD}, \text{GD}, \text{TRT}, \text{Sent}\}$. Because Eq.~(1) uses zero text labels, it is 100\% applicable to unseen test subjects during calibration or streaming operation, removing subject-specific $1/f$ spectral slopes and skull-impedance scaling.

\subsubsection{3D Stereotactic 105-to-12 Cortical ROI Grouping}
Using the 3D Cartesian coordinates $\{(X_c, Y_c, Z_c)\}_{c=1}^{105}$ from \texttt{chanlocs105.mat}, we partition the 105 scalp electrodes into $R=12$ bilateral functional-anatomical regions essential to visual-orthographic, lexical-semantic, and syntactic processing: Left/Right Prefrontal, Left Broca/IFG and Right IFG, Left/Right Central, Left Wernicke/Temporal and Right Temporal, Left/Right Parietal, and Left/Right Occipital (splitting midline electrodes $|Y_c| < 0.05$ symmetrically $0.5/0.5$ across hemispheres). Given assignment matrix $\mathbf{A} \in \mathbb{R}_{\ge 0}^{12 \times 105}$ and normalized 3D ROI centroids $\mathbf{C}_{\text{3D}} \in [-1,1]^{12 \times 3}$, artifact-free regional power is obtained via masked weighted pooling:
\begin{equation}
    \mathbf{Z}_{\text{ROI}}^{(\omega)} = \frac{(\tilde{\mathbf{X}}^{(\omega)} \odot \mathbf{m}_{\text{ch}}^\top)\mathbf{A}^\top}{\mathbf{m}_{\text{ch}}^\top \mathbf{A}^\top + \epsilon} \in \mathbb{R}^{8 \times 12}.
\end{equation}

\subsubsection{Chronological Scanpath Ordering and Four-Stage Node Features}
Human readers frequently skip predictable words and execute regressive saccades back to syntactically ambiguous words. Whereas prior pipelines sort word EEG strictly in left-to-right lexical order, we sort fixated words according to their true chronological first-fixation timestamp $t_{\text{scan}}$ from \texttt{word.fixPositions}. For each scanpath token $t$, we construct a $488$-dimensional structured node vector $\mathbf{v}_t \in \mathbb{R}^{488}$ concatenating:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Four-Stage Cortical Power ($384$-d):} Flattened $8 \times 12$ ROI tensors across early visual recognition ($\mathbf{Z}^{\text{FFD}}_t$), first-pass lexical access ($\mathbf{Z}^{\text{GD}}_t$), integrative reading ($\mathbf{Z}^{\text{TRT}}_t$), and explicit syntactic reanalysis cost ($\Delta\mathbf{Z}^{\text{re}}_t = \mathbf{Z}^{\text{TRT}}_t - \mathbf{Z}^{\text{FFD}}_t$).
    \item \textbf{Homologous Hemispheric Lateralization ($96$-d):} Left-minus-right ROI power differences across the 6 homologous region pairs for $\text{GD}$ and $\text{TRT}$, capturing left-lateralized perisylvian language dominance.
    \item \textbf{Oculomotor and Pupil Salience Metadata ($8$-d):} Log-transformed fixation durations, regression revisit counts, per-subject Z-scored pupil dilation (indexing locus-coeruleus cognitive arousal), normalized scanpath progress $u_t \in [0, 0.92]$, and scanpath-vs-lexical displacement $v_t - u_t$.
\end{itemize}
Following CET-MAE~\cite{wang2024cetmae}, a holistic sentence-level token is appended at the final active slot ($u_{L} = 1.0$).

\subsubsection{Pure Phase-Lag Connectivity and Riemannian Coupled Signal Strength}
To capture inter-regional coordination alongside regional activation amplitude, we project the continuous 500-Hz sentence waveform $\mathbf{X}_{\text{raw}}$ onto the 12 valid ROIs and compute multi-window Welch cross-spectral density matrices $\mathbf{S}_b(w, f) \in \mathbb{C}^{12 \times 12}$ (256-sample Hann windows, 50\% overlap) within each oscillation band $b$:
\begin{align}
    \text{wPLI}_b(u,v) &= \frac{\left|\mathbb{E}_{w,f}\!\left[\Im\big(S_{b,uv}(w,f)\big)\right]\right|}{\mathbb{E}_{w,f}\!\left[\left|\Im\big(S_{b,uv}(w,f)\big)\right|\right] + \epsilon}, \\
    \text{iCoh}_b(u,v) &= \frac{\left|\Im\big(\bar{S}_{b,uv}\big)\right|}{\sqrt{\Re(\bar{S}_{b,uu})\Re(\bar{S}_{b,vv})} + \epsilon},
\end{align}
yielding $66 \times 8 = 528$ pure phase-lagged connectivity features each for wPLI and iCoh that automatically reject zero-lag ($\Delta\phi=0$) volume conduction.

Because pure phase synchronization cannot distinguish weak background synchrony from high-energy coupled neural recruitment, we simultaneously extract \textit{Coupled Signal Strength} on the Symmetric Positive Definite ($\mathcal{S}_{++}^{12}$) manifold. Forming the shrinkage-regularized real band cross-covariance $\mathbf{P}_b = (1-\lambda)\Re(\bar{\mathbf{S}}_b) + \lambda \frac{\operatorname{tr}(\Re(\bar{\mathbf{S}}_b))}{12}\mathbf{I}_{12}$ ($\lambda=0.08$) with eigendecomposition $\mathbf{P}_b = \mathbf{U}_b \boldsymbol{\Sigma}_b \mathbf{U}_b^\top$, we map $\mathbf{P}_b$ onto the flat Log-Euclidean tangent space at the identity~\cite{arsigny2006logeuclidean}:
\begin{equation}
    \mathbf{T}_b = \operatorname{upper\_vec}\!\left(\mathbf{U}_b \log(\boldsymbol{\Sigma}_b) \mathbf{U}_b^\top\right) \in \mathbb{R}^{78},
\end{equation}
scaling off-diagonal entries by $\sqrt{2}$ to preserve Riemannian geodesic inner products. Concatenating $\text{wPLI}$ ($528$-d), $\text{iCoh}$ ($528$-d), $\mathbf{T}_{1:8}$ ($624$-d), and the 48-pair whole-sentence lateralization vector ($384$-d) produces a $2064$-dimensional global connectome descriptor $\mathbf{c}_{\text{sent}} \in \mathbb{R}^{2064}$ (alongside compact named clinical biomarkers exported for downstream explainable reasoning).

\subsection{Stage 1: Length-Invariant Late-Fusion CET-RVQ Pretraining}

\subsubsection{Eliminating Signal-Length Leakage via Continuous Progress PE}
Under conventional integer positional encoding $\operatorname{PE}(t)$ ($t \in \{0,\dots,L-1\}$), the highest active index directly reveals $L$ to downstream self- and cross-attention layers. We instead introduce \textbf{Normalized Scanpath Progress Fourier Encoding}: every trial's active word fixations are parameterized over the unit interval $u_t \in [0, 0.92]$ with holistic sentence summary at $u_L = 1.0$, regardless of whether a sentence contains 5 or 45 words. Given log-spaced frequencies $\omega_k = 2^k \pi$ ($k=0,\dots,15$), the positional embedding is computed over $[\sin(\boldsymbol{\omega} u_t), \cos(\boldsymbol{\omega} u_t), \sin(\boldsymbol{\omega} v_t), \cos(\boldsymbol{\omega} v_t), \tilde{\psi}_t]$, where $\tilde{\psi}_t$ is word fixation salience.

\subsubsection{3D-Grounded Node Transformer and Zero-Initialized Late Connectome Bottleneck Fusion}
In \texttt{Cortical3DROINodeStem}, each ROI's 32 stage-band features are projected and summed with a learned MLP embedding of its 3D coordinate $\mathbf{c}_{r} \in \mathbb{R}^3$ before entering an $L_{\text{enc}}$-layer Pre-LN Transformer (\texttt{embed\_dim} $D=768$) conditioned on task embedding $\mathbf{e}_{\tau}$ and oculomotor cognitive load $\mathbf{g}_{\text{cog}} \in \mathbb{R}^6$. A Perceiver-style \texttt{LatentQueryResampler} compresses the variable-length sequence into a fixed number $K=24$ of latent neural queries $\mathbf{H}_q \in \mathbb{R}^{K \times D}$.

Crucially, our ablation study (Section~\ref{sec:ablation}) reveals that injecting high-dimensional pairwise edge features directly into every word token (\textit{early token-level fusion}) induces cross-subject overfitting on limited training subjects. We therefore introduce a \textbf{Late Connectome Bottleneck Fusion Adapter}: $\mathbf{c}_{\text{sent}}$ is decomposed into 4 specialized neuro-physical tokens (wPLI, iCoh, SPD-Tangent, and L-R Asymmetry), projected into a low-rank bottleneck ($D_{\text{bot}}=128$, dropout $0.30$), and fused into $\mathbf{H}_q$ via cross-attention with a zero-initialized output projection $\mathbf{W}_{\text{up}} = \mathbf{0}$:
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
    \tilde{\mathbf{H}}_q &= \mathbf{H}_q \\
    &\quad + \mathbf{W}_{\text{up}} \operatorname{CrossAttn}\!\left(\mathbf{H}_q \mathbf{W}_Q,\, \mathbf{C}_{\text{glob}}\mathbf{W}_K,\, \mathbf{C}_{\text{glob}}\mathbf{W}_V\right).
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
At initialization, $\tilde{\mathbf{H}}_q = \mathbf{H}_q$, guaranteeing clean spatio-temporal node representations while allowing global phase-lag and Riemannian manifold context to refine latent queries where cross-subject generalization benefits.

\subsubsection{Un-Inflated Coarse-to-Fine EMA-RVQ}
To discretize $\tilde{\mathbf{H}}_q$ without codebook collapse or residual norm explosion, we project each query onto the unit hypersphere \textbf{once} ($\mathbf{r}^{(0)} = \tilde{\mathbf{h}}_q / \|\tilde{\mathbf{h}}_q\|_2$) and quantize decaying residuals across $M=6$ codebooks of size $V=256$ (\texttt{EuclideanEMAQuantizerLayer}, decay $0.98$, dead-code revival threshold $1.0$):
\begin{align}
    \mathbf{z}_q^{(m)} &= \arg\min_{\mathbf{e} \in \mathcal{C}_m} \|\mathbf{r}^{(m-1)} - \mathbf{e}\|_2^2, \\
    \mathbf{r}^{(m)} &= \mathbf{r}^{(m-1)} - \operatorname{sg}\!\left(\mathbf{z}_q^{(m)}\right).
\end{align}
Unlike naive cosine-RVQ implementations that re-normalize tiny higher-stage residuals back to unit norm, preserving natural residual decay alongside \textbf{Quantizer Dropout} ($p_{\text{qdrop}} = 0.30$~\cite{zeghidour2021soundstream}) forces early codebooks to encode coarse sentence semantics and deeper codebooks to capture fine lexical nuances. Commitment loss is summed over feature dimensions ($\mathcal{L}_{\text{RVQ}} = \frac{1}{M'}\sum_{m=1}^{M'} \beta \mathbb{E}[\sum_{d}(r_d^{(m-1)} - \operatorname{sg}(z_{q,d}^{(m)}))^2]$) so its scale naturally matches contrastive gradients.

\subsubsection{Multi-Positive Cross-Subject InfoNCE and Neural Invariance Objective}
In multi-subject reading datasets, distinct subjects frequently read the identical sentence $y_i = y_j$. Standard instance-wise InfoNCE erroneously penalizes $(i,j)$ as negative pairs. Pairing a \texttt{SentencePairedBatchSampler} with a \textbf{Cognitive-Load-Weighted Multi-Positive InfoNCE} ($\mathcal{L}_{\text{MP-NCE}}$), we normalize target probability mass across all matching sentence IDs in a mini-batch, weighted by trial arousal/engagement $w_i = \operatorname{clip}(1 + 0.20\tilde{p}_i + 0.15\rho_{\text{reg},i} - 0.25\rho_{\text{skip},i}, 0.65, 1.45)$ with clamped temperature $\tau^{-1} \le 14.29$. Furthermore, we add a \textbf{Cross-Subject Brain-to-Brain Invariance Loss} ($\mathcal{L}_{\text{B2B}}$) pulling quantized embeddings of different subjects reading the same sentence together, an asymmetric 75\%-masked node reconstruction loss ($\mathcal{L}_{\text{MAE}}$), and an auxiliary sentiment head ($\mathcal{L}_{\text{Aux}}$):
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
    \mathcal{L}_{\text{Stage1}} &= \lambda_{\text{MAE}}\mathcal{L}_{\text{MAE}} + \lambda_{\text{RVQ}}\mathcal{L}_{\text{RVQ}} + \lambda_{\text{Con}}\mathcal{L}_{\text{MP-NCE}} \\
    &\quad + \lambda_{\text{B2B}}\mathcal{L}_{\text{B2B}} + \lambda_{\text{Aux}}\mathcal{L}_{\text{Aux}}.
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}

\subsection{Stage 2: Exposure-Bias-Mitigated Causal LLM Decoding}
In Stage~2, quantized brain tokens $\mathbf{Z}_q \in \mathbb{R}^{K \times D}$ are mapped into the embedding space of \texttt{Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct}~\cite{qwen25} ($D_{\text{LLM}} = 3584$) via a 2-layer MLP \texttt{BrainToLLMProjector} and wrapped inside an instruction prompt prefix. Low-Rank Adaptation (LoRA, $r=16, \alpha=32$)~\cite{hu2022lora} is applied to all attention and MLP projections while jointly fine-tuning the neural encoder at a conservative learning rate ($3\times 10^{-5}$).

To prevent the 7B autoregressive decoder from ignoring the neural prefix and relying solely on preceding ground-truth words during training (\textit{exposure bias}), we apply \textbf{Linearly Scheduled Target-Word Dropout} up to $p_{\text{drop}} = 0.50$: half of the input target embeddings are randomly masked with \texttt{$<$pad$>$} during causal LM training while the cross-entropy loss still requires predicting every true next token from the quantized brain prefix. At test time, generation is strictly \textbf{teacher-forcing-free} via beam search ($B=4$, no-repeat 3-gram constraint) conditioned solely on the neural prefix, paired with an automated Gaussian noise input sanity audit where both feature values and sequence masks are randomized.

\section{Experimental Setup}

\subsection{Datasets and Strict Subject-Independent Partitioning}
We evaluate RUSH-EEG2Text across four naturalistic reading tasks from \textbf{ZuCo 1.0}~\cite{hollenstein2018zuco} and \textbf{ZuCo 2.0}~\cite{hollenstein2020zuco2}: Movie Sentiment Reading (\texttt{task1-SR}, 400 sentences, 12 subjects), Wikipedia Normal Reading v1 (\texttt{task2-NR}, 300 sentences, 12 subjects), Task-Specific Relation Reading (\texttt{task3-TSR}, 407 sentences, 12 subjects), and Wikipedia Normal Reading v2 (\texttt{task2-NR-2.0}, 349 sentences, 18 subjects; corrupted subject \texttt{YMH} excluded per protocol~\cite{wang2024cetmae}). Parallel 16-worker preprocessing across all 54 MATLAB/HDF5 archives yields \textbf{18,276} complete Dual-Stream Connectome trials across \textbf{30 unique human subjects} and \textbf{1,313 unique sentences}.

To guarantee zero subject leakage:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Controlled 11-Subject Stage~1 Ablation Benchmark (\texttt{task1-SR}, 4,140 trials):} 9 subjects ($3,416$ trials) for training, unseen subject \texttt{ZJS} ($338$ trials) for validation/hyperparameter selection, and unseen subject \texttt{ZMG} ($386$ trials) strictly held out for testing against the full 400-sentence candidate bank (random chance: $\text{R@1} = 0.25\%$, $\text{R@10} = 2.50\%$, $\text{MRR} = 1.64\%$).
    \item \textbf{Full-Scale 4-Task Benchmark (18,276 trials, 30 subjects):} 24 subjects ($14,297$ trials) for training, 2 unseen subjects (\texttt{ZJS}, \texttt{YDR}; $1,262$ trials) for validation, and 4 unseen subjects spanning both corpora (\texttt{ZMG}, \texttt{ZPH}, \texttt{YTL}, \texttt{YSL}; $2,717$ trials) for final evaluation.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Hardware Footprint and Reproducible Implementation}
All preprocessing, Stage~1 optimization sweeps, and Stage~2 7B LLM fine-tuning were executed on a single desktop \textbf{NVIDIA DGX Spark} workstation equipped with an NVIDIA GB10 Grace Blackwell Superchip (20-core Arm CPU + Blackwell Tensor Core GPU sharing 128~GB coherent LPDDR5x unified memory, PyTorch~2.7, CUDA~13.0, native \texttt{BF16}). Stage~1 full 4-task training consumes $\sim 15.7$~GB of unified memory ($38.9$~min for 55 epochs), Stage~2 joint batch-32 end-to-end LoRA training consumes $\sim 66.1$~GB ($\sim 21.4$~GB at batch-8 / inference), demonstrating that generative EEG-LLM pipelines can be trained and deployed locally inside clinical neurology departments without cloud data transfer.

Table~\ref{tab:stage1_ablation} summarizes our systematic 14-run controlled ablation on \texttt{task1-SR}. Four major scientific findings emerge:

\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Proof and Elimination of Fixation-Length Leakage (\texttt{R0} vs.\ \texttt{R1a}):} In baseline \texttt{R0}, replacing real EEG with $\mathcal{N}(0,\mathbf{I})$ still yielded $5.29\%$ MRR ($3.2\times$ random chance), leaving a meager $+1.11\%$ real-over-noise gap. Switching to continuous $[0,1]$ scanpath-progress Fourier encoding paired with multi-positive cross-subject InfoNCE (\texttt{R1a}) immediately forced Gaussian noise down to true random chance ($1.55\%$ MRR) while raising real unseen-subject MRR to $10.52\%$ ($+8.97\%$ sanity gap).
    \item \textbf{Massive Gain from 3D 12-ROI Parcellation and Four-Stage Scanpath Dynamics (\texttt{R1a} $\rightarrow$ \texttt{R1d}):} Replacing flat 840-d single-window GD vectors with 3D-grounded 12-ROI four-stage power ($\text{FFD}, \text{GD}, \text{TRT}, \text{TRT}-\text{FFD}$), hemispheric asymmetry, chronological gaze ordering, and pupil cognitive-load weighting nearly doubled unseen-subject test MRR from $10.52\%$ to $20.18\%$ ($\text{R@10}: 26.94\% \rightarrow 40.93\%$).
    \item \textbf{Why Late Connectome Bottleneck Beats Early Edge Concatenation (\texttt{R1c}/\texttt{R5a} vs.\ \texttt{R5c}):} Injecting 528-d pairwise co-fluctuation edges into every word token (\texttt{R1c}, \texttt{R5a}) caused the Transformer to overfit training-subject correlation patterns ($14.94\%\text{--}16.54\%$ Test MRR). Conversely, our zero-initialized \textit{Late Connectome Bottleneck Fusion} (\texttt{R5c})---which lets the 3D Node Transformer extract clean spatiotemporal word dynamics before querying compressed global wPLI, iCoh, and Log-Euclidean SPD tokens---achieved the highest unseen-subject generalization across every metric on \texttt{ZMG}: $\mathbf{12.18\%}$ R@1 ($48.7\times$ random chance), $\mathbf{30.83\%}$ R@5, $\mathbf{44.30\%}$ R@10, median rank $\mathbf{15.0}$ out of 400 sentences, and a $+\mathbf{20.80\%}$ real-vs-noise sanity gap.
    \item \textbf{Diagnostic Controls (Gaze-Only, EEG-Shuffle, Length-Matched Noise, and Continuous Holistic EEG):} To rigorously address whether RUSH-EEG2Text relies genuinely on neuroelectrical signals rather than eye-tracking cues or sequence length, we evaluated three diagnostic controls alongside fixation-free EEG:
    \begin{itemize}
        \item \textit{Gaze-Only Control (\texttt{C1}):} Zeroing all 3D cortical ROI power and functional connectomes while preserving true word-level oculomotor/pupil metadata drops Test MRR from $18.82\%$ to $9.15\%$ ($\text{R@10}: 44.56\% \rightarrow 22.02\%$), establishing a massive $+\mathbf{9.67\%}$ MRR ($+\mathbf{22.54\%}$ R@10) gain attributable purely to EEG brain dynamics.
        \item \textit{EEG-Shuffle Control (\texttt{C2}):} Permuting EEG feature tensors across random trials while preserving original gaze lengths and target alignments reduces Test MRR to $15.76\%$ ($\text{R@10}=35.23\%$), confirming that trials require trial-aligned neural waveforms ($+\mathbf{3.06\%}$ $\Delta\text{MRR}$ over shuffled EEG).
        \item \textit{Length-Matched Noise Control (\texttt{C3}):} Replacing EEG with Gaussian noise matched to true sequence lengths yields only $2.49\%$ MRR ($5.44\%$ R@10), demonstrating that continuous $[0,1]$ Fourier PE prevents length exploitation even when token counts match.
        \item \textit{Oculomotor Feature Ablation (\texttt{R5e}) vs.\ Continuous Holistic EEG (\texttt{R5f}):} Zeroing all 8-d oculomotor metadata (\texttt{R5e}) maintains $\mathbf{18.39\%}$ R@10 and $\mathbf{7.38\%}$ MRR on \texttt{ZMG} ($+\mathbf{15.80\%}$ R@10 over noise), notably outperforming the baseline \texttt{R0} ($12.73\%$ R@10) which had access to full gaze features. However, when word-level temporal segmentation is entirely removed in 100\% fixation-free continuous holistic mode (\texttt{R5f}, $L_{\text{seq}}=1$), retrieval performance drops to $2.59\%$ R@10 ($2.03\%$ MRR), which is statistically indistinguishable from 400-way random chance ($2.50\%$, $p=0.41$). This crucial negative finding demonstrates that while localized ROI power and phase connectomes provide strong decoding signals, precise temporal boundary discovery remains a foundational open challenge for continuous, gaze-free BCI translation.
    \end{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Lossless Multi-Stage Discrete Quantization and Per-Subject Stability Across 30 Subjects (18,276 Trials, 1,313-Way Retrieval):} When scaled to all four reading tasks across ZuCo~1.0 and ZuCo~2.0 (24 training subjects, 2 unseen validation subjects, and 4 strictly unseen test subjects: \texttt{ZMG}, \texttt{ZPH}, \texttt{YTL}, \texttt{YSL}), Stage~1 achieves \textbf{$\mathbf{60.62\%}$ Val R@10} ($\text{MRR}=\mathbf{25.63\%}$, median rank $\mathbf{8.0}$) and \textbf{$\mathbf{56.09\%}$ Overall Unseen-Test R@10} ($\text{R@1}=\mathbf{9.94\%}$ [$130\times$ random chance of $0.076\%$], $\text{R@5}=\mathbf{35.04\%}$, $\text{MRR}=\mathbf{23.14\%}$, median rank $\mathbf{9.0}$ out of 1,313 sentences), with a negligible quantization gap of only $0.83\%$ MRR across all 6 discrete codebooks (per-layer perplexities: $[20.7, 55.9, 82.1, 94.4, 100.3, 74.1]$). Crucially, Table~\ref{tab:per_subject_comprehensive} confirms that cross-subject generalization is highly uniform across all four held-out individuals spanning both recording corpora ($\text{R@10} = \mathbf{56.79 \pm 2.44\%}$, $\text{MRR} = \mathbf{23.09 \pm 1.64\%}$, median rank $= \mathbf{8.50 \pm 0.58}$): ZuCo~2.0 subject \texttt{YSL} ($24.91\%$ MRR, $58.89\%$ R@10) and ZuCo~1.0 subject \texttt{ZPH} ($23.56\%$ MRR) both exceed \texttt{ZMG} ($22.90\%$ MRR), and every individual subject clears a $>+19.8\%$ MRR sanity gap over pure Gaussian noise.
\end{enumerate}

Table~\ref{tab:stage2_generation} compares RUSH-EEG2Text against published cross-subject baselines and pure-noise diagnostic audits across 2,717 held-out trials from 4 completely unseen test subjects (\texttt{ZMG}, \texttt{ZPH}, \texttt{YTL}, \texttt{YSL}). Three critical results stand out:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Empirical Reproduction and Resolution of the Noise-Memorization Trap:} Our diagnostic Ablation Row~1 reproduces the exact failure mode exposed by Jo \textit{et al.}~\cite{jo2024are}: when Stage~1 suffers from vanilla VQ collapse (perplexity $<2.3$), a 7B LLM adapter achieves seemingly plausible unigram overlap ($\text{BLEU-1}=10.84\%, \text{ROUGE-1}=13.42\%$) solely by repeating generic corpus templates (\textit{``It's a film that will make you think...''}), yielding $\Delta\text{BLEU-1} = -0.66\%$ against pure Gaussian noise.
    \item \textbf{Decisive Positive Neural Sanity Gap Under Strict TF-Free Decoding:} Pairing our length-invariant Stage~1 Champion (\texttt{R5c}) with 50\% scheduled target-word dropout and joint LoRA adaptation elevates unseen-subject validation accuracy to $22.15\%$ BLEU-1 ($12.21\%$ BLEU-4, $88.70\%$ WER at Epoch~8) and achieves $\mathbf{19.60\%}$ Test BLEU-1, $\mathbf{12.82\%}$ BLEU-2, $\mathbf{10.81\%}$ BLEU-3, $\mathbf{9.89\%}$ BLEU-4 ($11.1\times$ higher than LURS~\cite{park2026lurs} and $19.0\times$ higher than CET-MAE/E2T-PTR~\cite{wang2024cetmae} under Non-TF cross-subject decoding), $\mathbf{19.58\%}$ ROUGE-1 F1 ($\text{P}=19.71\%, \text{R}=19.50\%$), and $\mathbf{90.85\%}$ WER. When test inputs and sequence masks are replaced with randomized Gaussian noise $\mathcal{N}(0,\mathbf{I})$, BLEU-4 collapses by $23.0\times$ to $0.43\%$ and WER doubles to $182.92\%$, establishing a $+\mathbf{10.83\%}$ $\Delta\text{BLEU-1}$ and $+\mathbf{9.46\%}$ $\Delta\text{BLEU-4}$ neural sanity gap.
    \item \textbf{Controlled Single-Task Baseline on Identical Backbone Family (BART-large 406M):} To isolate architectural discrete-connectome gains from language model parameter scale (\texttt{Qwen2.5-7B} vs.\ \texttt{BART-large}) and multi-task training corpus size (18,276 trials vs.\ 3,809 trials), we trained a controlled baseline pairing Stage~1 Champion (\texttt{R5c}) with \texttt{facebook/bart-large} (406M) strictly on single-task \texttt{task1-SR} under the exact same non-teacher-forced protocol. As detailed in Table~\ref{tab:stage2_generation}, RUSH-EEG2Text-BART achieves $\mathbf{16.38\%}$ BLEU-1, $\mathbf{4.83\%}$ BLEU-2, $\mathbf{2.01\%}$ BLEU-3, $\mathbf{0.97\%}$ BLEU-4, $\mathbf{17.17\%}$ ROUGE-1 F1, $\mathbf{100.32\%}$ WER, and $\mathbf{21.62\%}$ centered contextual cosine similarity ($+20.04\%$ over noise). Crucially, even on the identical 406M backbone family and single-task corpus, RUSH-EEG2Text outperforms CET-MAE~\cite{wang2024cetmae} ($13.55\%$ BLEU-1, $0.52\%$ BLEU-4) and LURS~\cite{park2026lurs} ($15.39\%$ BLEU-1, $0.89\%$ BLEU-4) across every single metric, confirming that our discrete connectome tokenizer provides genuine representational advantages independent of LLM capacity.
    \item \textbf{Inter-Subject Generalization Stability Across Held-Out Individuals:} Evaluating Stage~2 generation across all four held-out test subjects individually confirms high cross-subject stability ($\text{BLEU-1} = 19.62 \pm 0.58\%$, $\text{BLEU-4} = 9.98 \pm 0.93\%$, $\text{ROUGE-1 F1} = 19.57 \pm 0.64\%$, $\text{WER} = 90.68 \pm 0.87\%$, $\text{Semantic CosSim} = 26.78 \pm 9.43\%$): ZuCo~2.0 subject \texttt{YSL} ($11.15\%$ BLEU-4, $19.94\%$ ROUGE-1), ZuCo~1.0 subject \texttt{ZPH} ($10.31\%$ BLEU-4, $20.28\%$ ROUGE-1), \texttt{ZMG} ($9.33\%$ BLEU-4, $18.99\%$ ROUGE-1), and \texttt{YTL} ($9.14\%$ BLEU-4, $19.08\%$ ROUGE-1) all achieve consistent, robust generation. Every individual subject decisively clears the Gaussian noise baseline ($\Delta\text{BLEU-4} = +9.54 \pm 0.61\%$), confirming that the model generalizes robustly rather than relying on a single high-SNR participant.
    \item \textbf{Qualitative Fidelity and Error Modes:} As shown in Table~\ref{tab:qualitative_examples}, when quantized 12-ROI connectome tokens fall within the high-confidence retrieval manifold across unseen subjects (\texttt{ZMG, ZPH, YTL, YSL}), the decoder reconstructs complex multi-clause sentences verbatim without any teacher-forced prefix; on lower-SNR trials, errors gracefully degrade toward polarity-consistent paraphrases rather than repetitive hallucinations.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Discussion and Limitations}
\textbf{Neurophysiological Interpretability and Bridge to Clinical Reasoning:} Because RUSH-EEG2Text explicitly separates localized multi-stage cortical activation ($\text{FFD} \rightarrow \text{GD} \rightarrow \text{TRT}$) from non-zero-lag phase synchronization (wPLI/iCoh) and Riemannian coupled power ($\log_m(\mathbf{P}_b)$), every trial exports human-interpretable network biomarkers---including Broca--Wernicke $\theta/\beta$ wPLI, occipito-temporal $\gamma$ coupling, frontal $\alpha$ asymmetry, and syntactic reanalysis indices. In companion work (\textit{NeuroReasoner}), we leverage these exact privacy-preserving biomarkers to ground chain-of-thought clinical assessments across multi-center psychiatric and neurodegenerative cohorts without exposing raw patient brainwaves.

\textbf{Cross-Subject Neural Indexing vs.\ Strict Unseen-Sentence Semantic Decoding:} Following established subject-independent ZuCo evaluation protocols~\cite{wang2024cetmae, park2026lurs}, unseen test subjects in our primary benchmark read from the shared task stimulus pool ($\mathcal{S}_{\text{train}} \cap \mathcal{S}_{\text{test}} = \emptyset$). Consequently, high exact phrase-level BLEU-4 ($9.89\%$) reflects high-precision cross-subject neural-to-semantic indexing into learned linguistic structures. To empirically disentangle cross-subject indexing from open-vocabulary semantic generalization, we conducted a strict \textit{double zero-shot} experiment isolating both unseen subjects and an 80/20 disjoint sentence pool ($\mathcal{S}_{\text{train}} \cap \mathcal{S}_{\text{test}} = \emptyset \land \mathcal{Y}_{\text{train}} \cap \mathcal{Y}_{\text{test}} = \emptyset$). By partitioning unique sentences via deterministic hashing, the test candidate bank contains $K_{\text{cand}} = 131$ completely novel sentences ($N=271$ trials across 4 held-out subjects). Crucially, 131-way retrieval cannot be directly compared against the 1,313-way benchmark: random chance in a 131-sentence bank is $\text{R@10} = 7.63\%$ and $\text{R@1} = 0.76\%$. As detailed in Table~\ref{tab:double_zeroshot}, Stage~1 achieves $\mathbf{45.39\%}$ R@10 ($5.9\times$ random chance, $\text{MRR}=\mathbf{18.26\%}$), while Stage~2 causal decoding maintains a robust positive sanity gap: $\mathbf{17.74\%}$ BLEU-1 ($+\mathbf{7.16\%}$ over Gaussian noise), $\mathbf{18.12\%}$ ROUGE-1 F1 ($+\mathbf{3.65\%}$ over noise), and $\mathbf{24.05\%}$ centered contextual semantic cosine similarity ($+\mathbf{9.18\%}$ over noise, $p<0.001$). As expected, exact 4-gram string overlap on novel vocabulary drops to $\text{BLEU-4}=0.28\%$ (comparable to noise $0.56\%$), and 3-class affective polarity agreement remains near chance ($33.87\%$ vs.\ $33.33\%$ chance and $30.65\%$ noise). These results demonstrate that on unseen sentences, the decoder generates semantically coherent conceptual paraphrases rather than memorized surface strings.

\textbf{Limitations and Future Roadmap for Continuous / Locked-In EEG:} While oculomotor metadata (chronological fixation order and pupil arousal) enhances lexical alignment during natural reading, individuals with complete locked-in syndrome (CLIS) or severe oculomotor palsy lack gaze control. As shown in Table~\ref{tab:stage1_ablation}, zeroing all oculomotor features (\texttt{R5e}) maintains $\mathbf{18.39\%}$ R@10 on \texttt{ZMG} ($+\mathbf{15.80\%}$ over noise), outperforming baseline \texttt{R0} ($12.73\%$). However, when all fixation segmentation is removed in continuous holistic sentence mode (\texttt{R5f}, $L_{\text{seq}}=1$), performance drops to $2.59\%$ R@10, which is statistically indistinguishable from 400-way random chance ($2.50\%$, $p=0.41$). We therefore explicitly refrain from claiming that current models can immediately translate continuous, unsegmented EEG in locked-in patients; rather, discovering self-supervised neural event boundaries directly from continuous 500-Hz waveforms remains an open frontier for non-invasive language BCIs.

\section{Conclusion}
We presented \textbf{RUSH-EEG2Text}, a Riemannian connectome-guided hierarchical quantization framework that eliminates fixation-length leakage and codebook collapse while enabling strict teacher-forcing-free language decoding on compact unified-memory hardware. Combining 3D 12-ROI four-stage reading dynamics, Late Connectome Bottleneck Fusion over Welch wPLI/iCoh and Log-Euclidean SPD tangent vectors, and un-inflated 6-book EMA-RVQ yielded a $3.47\times$ improvement in unseen-subject 400-way retrieval MRR ($22.22\%$ vs.\ $6.40\%$), $56.09\%$ 1,313-way unseen-subject R@10 across 30 subjects, and $19.60\%$ BLEU-1 / $9.89\%$ BLEU-4 ($+10.83\%$ / $+9.46\%$ over Gaussian noise) in strict teacher-forcing-free generation. Controlled single-task experiments on \texttt{BART-large} ($16.38\%$ BLEU-1, $0.97\%$ BLEU-4) and double zero-shot evaluations ($24.05\%$ semantic similarity, $+9.18\%$ over noise) confirm that RUSH-EEG2Text provides genuine representational advantages independent of LLM capacity.
